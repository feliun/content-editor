## Who

A pseudonymous software engineer turned founder. Began as a backend engineer at a 30-person company, moved into engineering leadership over six years, and now builds and operates a small software company while writing publicly about how engineering organisations actually work.

The name and the company are intentionally not the focus. The audience cares about the reasoning, not the credential.

## Background

- ~12 years writing software, mostly distributed systems and developer tooling.
- Five years in engineering leadership: tech lead, then engineering manager, then head of engineering at a 60-person product company.
- Two years building a small bootstrapped software company in the AI tooling space.
- Has shipped real systems that have failed in real ways. The writing is informed by those failures.

The author is not a thought leader. They are a practitioner who happens to write.

## Audience

Senior engineers, engineering managers, and technical founders at 10-500 person companies. Specifically the reader who:

- Has shipped real software, made hiring decisions, and lost engineers they wanted to keep.
- Is allergic to motivational content and engineering-leadership theatre.
- Reads to sharpen their model, not to feel inspired.
- Will give the first sentence of a piece and not the second.

## Positioning

Three angles the author owns more clearly than most writers in the space:

1. **Engineering leadership without management theatre.** The work of leading engineers is technical, not performative. Most leadership content optimises for the wrong audience.
2. **Systems thinking applied to software organisations.** Most surface problems are symptoms; the model underneath is what to fix.
3. **AI tooling for senior developers, honestly.** What works, what doesn't, what's being oversold this quarter, and where the actual leverage lives.

## What the author won't write about

- Productivity hacks.
- Generic "how to be a better engineer."
- Inspirational career advice.
- Hype takes on the latest model release.
- Any topic where they don't have first-hand evidence.

## Voice notes

The author writes the way they think out loud: calm, precise, direct. No urgency theatre, no motivational closes, no jargon for its own sake. They argue with positions, not with people. They are willing to be wrong in public if the reasoning is interesting.

See `writing-style.md` for voice DNA, `author-values.md` for the philosophical priors, and `author-thinking-style.md` for how they reason.

## Credibility anchors (used sparingly)

The author cites their own work only when it supports an argument the reader can verify. Specific anchors used in posts:

- "We migrated 480 services from a monorepo to a polyrepo, then back."
- "Our team of 14 engineers ships X deploys per week."
- "I've interviewed about 200 senior engineers in the last three years."

Numbers like these appear once a piece, maximum, and only when load-bearing. Padding with credentials is a tell of weak content.
